# Prospects

Who the program is for, with real, publicly identifiable examples. **Public information
only.** Every route below is an organization's own contact page, a generic address it
publishes itself, or its public forum. No individuals' names, personal addresses or
personal accounts appear here, and none should be added. Conversation notes go in the
private tracker.

**Nobody on this list has been contacted.** Being listed here is not a sign that a
community wants to hear from us. The outreach rules are in
[`outreach-templates.md`](outreach-templates.md).

Researched and checked 2026-10-13: every URL in the tables loaded (HTTP 200), and
Discord member counts come from Discord's public invite preview, read without joining.
Re-check a route the day before using it; contact pages move.

## Community types that fit

Ranked by how well Ohiyo fits them **today**, given its limits.

| # | Type | Why they'd want it | What makes it hard | Where to find them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hackerspaces and makerspaces** | Technical members who can self-host on hardware they already own; text-first coordination; webhooks for door bots and CI; often uneasy with corporate platforms | Members sign up together from one network (the sign-up limit); some are large | [hackerspaces.org list](https://wiki.hackerspaces.org/List_of_Hacker_Spaces) (bot check; open in a browser), [EFF Electronic Frontier Alliance](https://www.eff.org/electronic-frontier-alliance/allies) |
| 2 | **Linux and BSD user groups** | Value open source and the AGPL; can read the code; Linux desktop app | Many already run Matrix or IRC and will compare | EFF EFA list, university club pages |
| 3 | **Campus CTF and security clubs** | Discord-heavy today; teams want private DMs and group chats for solutions; will run their own instance | Clubs are often large; members will test it hard (send them to private vulnerability reporting, not our hosted service) | [CTFtime rankings](https://ctftime.org/stats/2026/US), [DEF CON Groups](https://defcongroups.org/), EFF EFA list |
| 4 | **Self-hosted open-source projects** | Their users already self-host; a support space they control fits their message | Popular projects have thousands of members on Discord; template import doesn't bring support history | [awesome-selfhosted](https://awesome-selfhosted.net/) (stars, last update and licence on every entry) |
| 5 | **Open-source and privacy podcasts** | Listener communities that like trying tools; a good public story if it works | Often already on Matrix or IRC; audiences are spread across platforms | Show pages' "community" links |
| 6 | **Small private game groups and open-source games** | Voice, screen share and watch parties without Nitro | Voice rooms over about five people need self-hosted LiveKit; groups rarely have a public organizational contact | Open-source game sites; partner referrals |
| 7 | **Digital-rights local groups** | Care about who holds their data; will read the threat model | Many coordinate by mailing list, not chat; members facing real risk need SimpleX or Signal, not Ohiyo | EFF EFA list |
| 8 | **Tech co-ops** | Values match (open source, owning infrastructure) | Usually on Discourse or Nextcloud, not Discord. Better as **hosting partners** that run Ohiyo for others than as design partners | [CoTech members](https://www.coops.tech/) |

Not a fit, whatever the type: groups that need anonymity or source protection,
regulated data, end-to-end encrypted channels, communities for children, or native phone
apps (see the [stop signals](qualification.md#stop-signals)).

## Suggested first wave

Strongest fits first, mixed types so we learn about more than one kind of community:

1. HeatSync Labs: whole-community fit.
2. LUG @ NC State: one project or semester cohort.
3. Dawarich: contributors' or support space.
4. Noisebridge: one working group.
5. Hack@UCF: one CTF team.

Hold the rest until the first wave has answered. Eight partners is the cap, and a
subgroup counts as one.
