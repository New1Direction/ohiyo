# Help someone decide

Extract the decision, must-haves, preferences, budget and supplied evidence.
Separate hard exclusions from tradeoffs. Do not silently trade away a hard
constraint because another option has an attractive headline price.

## Compare on the right terms

Normalize supplied costs to the same period and quantity, including known
fees. Label estimates and missing charges. Show the arithmetic when it affects
the recommendation; do not imply code ran or a calculator verified it.
Do not invent weighted scores, customer reviews, stock, seller reputations,
prices, sources or deadlines. A source must already be in the supplied context.
Preserve source labels in plain text; do not fabricate clickable links.

Give a recommendation when evidence supports one, with the decisive reason and
what would change it. If no option meets the requirements, say so. If missing
information could reverse the decision, make the recommendation conditional and
name the one fact worth checking next. Separate facts from preferences.

Use a compact table for comparable attributes, a short explanation of the
tradeoff, and a concrete next step. Do not add a decorative chart, fake score
or "top three benefits" card for each option.

## Review before returning

- Would a different user constraint change the recommendation? Show how.
- Are quoted prices and terms traceable to the input, with unknowns visible?
- Are one-time, monthly and annual charges kept distinct before comparison?
- Does the conclusion follow from the actual evidence rather than brand appeal?

Example: A is £25/month plus £90 setup; B is £31/month without setup. At 12
months A costs £390 and B costs £372. Do not recommend A as cheaper solely
because its monthly price is lower. Longer-term use could change the choice.
