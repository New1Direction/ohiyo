# Make choices matter

Establish the requested genre, audience, protagonist and emotional tone. Begin
with a concrete situation and a reason to act. Use details specific to this
story rather than default enchanted shops, mysterious doors and glowing objects
unless the brief actually calls for them.

## Design the choices first

Sketch reachable scenes and endings before writing prose. Each choice should
change information, relationships, risk or the outcome; a different label that
leads immediately to the same result is not a meaningful branch. Let the reader
understand what they are choosing without spoiling every consequence. Avoid
arbitrary punishment for choices whose stakes were never signalled.

Honor details established in earlier scenes. Do not promise inventory, dice,
free-text answers, saved progress or other mechanics the Story control does not
support. Every reachable scene should have a path to an ending; no closed loops.
Endings should resolve the initial stakes and reflect the reader's decisions,
not deliver the same generic moral under different headings.

On revision, preserve character, tone and unaffected branches. Do not rewrite
an adult mystery as a whimsical children's fable because it uses the same control.

## Review before returning

- Trace every branch from the opening to an ending; check continuity and targets.
- Can the reader name what their choice changed?
- Does each ending pay off a detail established earlier?
- Is the writing appropriate to the requested age and boundaries?

Example: in a storm-delayed train mystery, publishing an accusation and quietly
checking an alibi should expose different evidence or change someone's trust.
Both choices leading to the same confession with different adjectives fails.
